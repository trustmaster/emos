**Scope: authored artifacts, not conversation.** This file sets the register of files
written for me — reports, reviews, docs, records. It does not set how you talk to me in
chat, and it never changes tool use, task scoping, or whether you confirm before acting.

## Posture

- Every sentence carries a claim, a number, or a decision. If it carries none of those, cut it.
- Flat by default. Enthusiasm is earned by the result, not supplied by the adjective.
- Name the system before the pattern. "Checkout's retry loop" before "the retry pattern".
- Say what I don't know in the same breath as what I do. Confidence and its limits travel together.
- Stop at the point the reader can act. The paragraph after that is almost always cuttable.

## Sentence mechanics

- **Connectors I use:** commas, periods, "so", "because", "which is why".
- **Connectors I don't:** "moreover", "furthermore", "that said", "in conclusion".
- **Signature construction:** state the number, then what it means. "p99 sat at 2.4s. That's
  four times our budget, and customers feel it at checkout."
- **Verb register:** plain and active. "We shipped the idempotency keys", never "the keys
  were shipped".
- **Person:** "we" for team work, "I" for judgments and asks. Never "one" or the passive dodge.
- **Punctuation:** no em-dashes. Semicolons rarely. Parentheses for asides only, never for
  the load-bearing clause.
- **Sentence length:** mostly 15 to 25 words. One short sentence per paragraph, on the point
  that matters.

## Document architecture

- **Opening:** the conclusion in the first two sentences. The argument follows it.
- **Ordering options:** recommendation first, then the runner-up and why it lost. Never a
  neutral survey that leaves the reader to decide what I already decided.
- **Non-goals and open questions:** always explicit. Silence reads as an oversight.
- **Ending:** the next action and its owner. No summary of what was just said.

## Numbers and uncertainty

- Estimates get a range and the assumption that drives it. "Two to three weeks, assuming the
  payments team frees up review time."
- Anything unverified gets its source and its age. "Per the June dashboard" is a different
  claim from "per last night's query".
- No false precision. "About 40%" unless something actually measured 38.7%.

## Formatting

- **Line wrapping:** hard-wrap prose at 90 characters.
- **Bold:** facts, numbers, and decisions. Never on connective prose or whole sentences.
- **Tables:** when there are three or more things compared on the same axes. Otherwise a list.
- **Lists:** bullets for parallel items, numbers only for real sequences.
- **Headings:** sentence case. No gerunds.

## Hard bans

**Words and phrases:** "delve", "leverage" as a verb, "robust", "seamless", "utilize",
"it's worth noting", "at the end of the day", "in today's fast-paced".

**Sentence shapes:**
- "Not X, it's Y." The corrective-reveal construction.
- Rule-of-three lists built for rhythm rather than because there are three things.
- A one-line paragraph used as a punchline.
- Rhetorical questions I then answer myself.

**Habits:** no emoji in written artifacts. No closing pep-talk. No "Great question."

## Self-check

Run this before writing the file:

1. Any banned word, phrase, or sentence shape? Rewrite it, don't soften it.
2. Does the opening state the conclusion, or warm up to it?
3. Is every bold span on a fact rather than a transition?
4. Can the last paragraph be cut with nothing lost? Cut it.
5. Does any sentence carry more than one idea? Split it.
6. Does anything here read as written by someone other than me? Fix that first.
